This project uses density functional theory (DFT) to investigate metal-enabled nucleophilic aromatic substitution (SNAr) as a strategy for selective arylation of cysteine residues 
on the Tau protein, a key target in Alzheimer’s disease research. Activation free energies in kcal/mol and structural descriptors were analyzed for a series of 
Fe, Ru, and Mn arene complexes to determine how metal coordination enhances reactivity. This method allows for biocompatability because without the metal complexes,
strong electron withdrawing groups and extremely harsh conditions not compatible with protiens and peptides are needed. Hierarchical and K-means clustering identified clear 
reactivity trends, showing that shorter metal–chlorine distances strongly correlated with improved reaction rates. These results provide computational design principles 
for developing selective covalent modifiers of Tau through metal-mediated arylation chemistry.
